# Hardware

The desk stand for the CrowPanel 1.46" rotary display: three printed parts, all generated by one parametric
CadQuery script. There are two versions of the shell and base. Pick one:

- **USB-C** (`usbc`): a USB-C breakout in the back wall, an IP5306 charge board and an 18350 cell inside. The stand
  runs on battery and charges, powers and flashes the display through its own port. Needs some soldering and the
  display's cable cut.
- **Cable** (`cable`): the display's own MX1.25-to-USB cable, uncut, leaves through a slot at the bottom of the back
  wall and is held by snap clips in the base. USB powered, no battery, no soldering.

The retainer ring is the same for both.

| File | Part |
| --- | --- |
| `crowpanel_stand_v5.py` | The source. Every dimension is a named parameter at the top; run it to rebuild the parts and print a set of fit and clash checks. `DESKLING_PORT=usbc` (default) or `DESKLING_PORT=cable` picks the version. Needs `pip install cadquery`. |
| `crowpanel_v5_shell_usbc.step` / `.stl` | The USB-C shell (65 × 91 × 53 mm). The display loads from below through a tilted bore and stops on a printed shoulder; a collar hides everything below its black grip band. USB-C port in the back wall, a hole in the right side for the battery board's button. Translucent PETG carries the display's LED skirt out as a glow. |
| `crowpanel_v5_shell_cable.step` / `.stl` | The cable shell: the same body with a 3.9 mm slot, open at the bottom, in the back wall, and no button hole. |
| `crowpanel_v5_base_usbc.step` / `.stl` | Base for the USB-C shell: pins for the breakout, rails for the IP5306 board, wire clips, the 18350 cradle. |
| `crowpanel_v5_base_cable.step` / `.stl` | Base for the cable shell: four snug clips along the cable's run, nothing else. |
| `crowpanel_v5_retainer.step` / `.stl` | The retainer ring, laid out to print (flange down; its front is trimmed flat to sit on the base, so it needs up to about 5 mm of support from the bed). Pushes the display's bottom plate up against the stop; 2 × M3×8 socket head screws through its ear into the shell. |
| `crowpanel_v5_retainer_assembled.step` | The same retainer, in its place in the stand. |
| `v4/`, `v3/` | Earlier versions. |

Both bases fix to their shell with 4 × M3×8 countersunk screws into heat-set inserts. The STEP files share one frame
(desk at z = 0, the front toward -Y) and line up when imported together. The shell STLs are moved down 3 mm onto the
bed, and the retainer files are in print orientation.

## What changed in v5

The knob turns freely with the retainer screwed tight. In v4 the turning parts of the display sat very close to the
shell: the lit skirt 0.20 mm under the lip and the grip band 0.25 mm inside the lip bore, while the retainer was
drawn 0.25 mm proud to press the display up against the stop. Any flex, a fixed base slightly shorter than Elecrow's
model, or the ring pulling harder at its screwed front than at its back pushed them into the lip, and the knob
dragged. v5 raises the lip's underside 0.4 mm (`LIP_A` 12.3 → 12.7), opens the lip bore and the bore around the
skirt (`LIP_R` 27.25 → 27.55, `SKIRT_CLR_R` 29.15 → 29.45) and cuts the preload to 0.10 mm (`RET_PRELOAD`). The
turning parts now clear the shell by 0.55–0.60 mm, and still clear it with the display tilted 1° on its stop. The
outside of the stand is unchanged, and the cable version is new.

## Electronics (USB-C version)

A 22 × 13 mm USB-C breakout (VBUS/GND/CC1/D-/D+/CC2, 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs fitted) at the rear port, an IP5306
charge + 5 V boost board (HW-775, 25 × 16 mm) on the right, an 18350 cell with 18650-holder contact plates on the
left. Wiring: breakout VBUS/GND → IP5306 VIN/GND; IP5306 BAT/GND → cell +/−. The display takes power and data
through the MX1.25-to-USB cable it ships with, plugged into its 4-pin USB connector (J37 on Elecrow's schematic:
1 VUSB, 2 D−, 3 D+, 4 GND, wired straight to the ESP32-S3's USB pins) with the USB end cut off: IP5306 OUT-5V/GND →
pins 1 and 4, breakout D−/D+ → pins 2 and 3. So the stand's USB-C port charges, powers and flashes it. Without a
battery, wire the breakout's four pads straight to the cable and leave out the IP5306.

## Measure first

`STOP_A` (7.0 mm) is the height of the display's fixed base above its bottom plate, taken from Elecrow's 3D model,
which has been wrong around the skirt before; measure yours before printing the shell. For the cable version,
measure the cable's jacket (`CABLE2_D`, 3.5 mm): the clips are drawn to grip it 0.05 mm under size. For the USB-C
version, the board sizes come from seller listings (±1–2 mm): check the breakout's hole spacing (`BRK_HOLE_X`) and
the IP5306 button's position (`KEY_IN`) against yours.

The website shows the parts in 3D (`site/pages/wiki/models.html`) and covers printing, wiring and assembly
(`site/pages/wiki/print.html`).
