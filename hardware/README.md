# Hardware

The desk stand for the CrowPanel 1.46" rotary display: two printed parts, a shell and a base, generated by one
parametric CadQuery script. Each comes in two versions. Pick one:

- **USB-C** (`usbc`): a USB-C breakout in the back wall, an IP5306 charge board and an 18350 cell inside. The stand
  runs on battery and charges, powers and flashes the display through its own port. Needs some soldering and the
  display's cable cut.
- **Cable** (`cable`): the display's own MX1.25-to-USB cable, uncut, leaves through a slot at the bottom of the back
  wall and is held by snap clips in the base. USB powered, no battery, no soldering.

| File | Part |
| --- | --- |
| `crowpanel_stand_v6.py` | The source. Every dimension is a named parameter at the top; run it to rebuild the parts and print a set of fit and clash checks. `DESKLING_PORT=usbc` (default) or `DESKLING_PORT=cable` picks the version. Needs `pip install cadquery`. |
| `crowpanel_v6_shell_usbc.step` / `.stl` | The USB-C shell (65 × 92 × 54 mm). The display presses in from below through a tilted bore and stops on a printed shoulder; a collar hides everything below its black grip band. USB-C port in the back wall, a hole in the right side for the battery board's button. Translucent PETG carries the display's LED skirt out as a glow. |
| `crowpanel_v6_shell_usbc_leds.step` / `.stl` | The USB-C shell with a charge window: one round-topped opening low in the right wall, from just in front of the IP5306's LED row back to its button, so you can see the charge LEDs (from the side, up to about 10° above level) and press the button with a fingertip. Takes the USB-C base. `DESKLING_LEDS=1` builds it. |
| `crowpanel_v6_shell_cable.step` / `.stl` | The cable shell: the same body with a 4.4 mm slot, open at the bottom, in the back wall, and no button hole. |
| `crowpanel_v6_base_usbc.step` / `.stl` | Base for the USB-C shell: three posts under the display, pins for the breakout, rails for the IP5306 board, cable clips, the 18350 cradle. |
| `crowpanel_v6_base_cable.step` / `.stl` | Base for the cable shell: the same three posts, and four snug clips along the cable's run. |
| `v5/`, `v4/`, `v3/` | Earlier versions. |

Each base fixes to its shell with 4 × M3×8 countersunk screws into heat-set inserts. Neither part needs supports.
The STEP files share one frame (desk at z = 0, the front toward -Y) and line up when imported together; the shell
STLs are moved down 3 mm onto the bed.

## What changed in v6

From a printed v5:

- **No retainer.** Screwed at its front ear only, it pulled away from the display at the back, and the display is
  already held well as a press fit in the shell. Three posts on the base now stand under the plain outer rim of
  the display's bottom plate (r 28.9–30 mm; the screw heads inside it stand 0.7 mm proud and are cleared by
  0.35 mm), one at the front and one each side, so it can't be pushed down into the stand. The posts rise into
  pockets in the shell as the base goes on. `STOP_GAP` sets how far below the plate they finish (0 = touching).
- **Collar.** In v5 the collar was a separate solid floating 0.3 mm above the cup rim: the lip moved up and the
  collar's base didn't, so slicers bridged a gap there and the wall round the groove printed badly. The collar
  now grows out of the cup at full radius, the wall round the lit skirt is 2.45 mm all the way up, and the
  outside groove is gone. Internal dimensions are unchanged.
- **Rear screw bosses.** The two at the back floated free of the shell in v4 and v5 (separate solids inside the
  rear cavity). They now run up to the roof.
- **Shell:** USB-C receptacle pocket and plug opening +0.25 mm a side; IP5306 button hole 1.6 mm further back
  (`KEY_IN` 5.1).
- **Base:** cradle 2 mm longer for protected 18350 cells (the extra goes forward, away from the breakout); 7 ×
  6.5 mm solder windows behind each battery contact; buttresses behind both contact walls, the rear inner one kept
  0.8 mm off the breakout's edge; the USB-C version's wire clips are now the same snug clips as the cable version,
  for the display's bare 4 mm cable (v5's were drawn for a 6 mm bundle); the breakout pins stand 1.5 mm proud, to
  melt over with a soldering iron; no pocket under the IP5306 button (it is SMD, top side).

## Electronics (USB-C version)

A 22 × 13 mm USB-C breakout (VBUS/GND/CC1/D-/D+/CC2, 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs fitted) at the rear port, an IP5306
charge + 5 V boost board (HW-775, 25 × 16 mm) on the right, an 18350 cell with 18650-holder contact plates on the
left. Wiring: breakout VBUS/GND → IP5306 VIN/GND; IP5306 BAT/GND → cell +/−. The display takes power and data
through the MX1.25-to-USB cable it ships with, plugged into its 4-pin USB connector (J37 on Elecrow's schematic:
1 VUSB, 2 D−, 3 D+, 4 GND, wired straight to the ESP32-S3's USB pins) with the USB end cut off: IP5306 OUT-5V/GND →
pins 1 and 4, breakout D−/D+ → pins 2 and 3. So the stand's USB-C port charges, powers and flashes it. Without a
battery, wire the breakout's four pads straight to the cable and leave out the IP5306.

The breakout sits on two pins; melt their tops over its holes. The IP5306 has no holes: double-sided foam tape
between its rails holds it and keeps its underside off the base.

## Measure first

`STOP_A` (7.0 mm) is the height of the display's fixed base above its bottom plate, taken from Elecrow's 3D model,
which has been wrong around the skirt before. It sets both the shoulder in the shell and the height of the posts in
the base: measure yours before printing. The display's cable is 4.0 mm across (`CABLE2_D`, measured); if yours differs, change it: the clips are drawn
to grip it 0.05 mm under size. For the USB-C version, the board sizes come from seller listings (±1–2 mm): check
the breakout's hole spacing (`BRK_HOLE_X`) and the IP5306 button's position (`KEY_IN`) against yours.

The website shows the parts in 3D (`site/pages/wiki/models.html`) and covers printing, wiring and assembly
(`site/pages/wiki/print.html`).
