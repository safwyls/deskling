# Hardware

The desk stand for the CrowPanel 1.46" rotary display, generated by one parametric CadQuery script. It comes three
ways:

- **USB-C** (`DESKLING_PORT=usbc`): a USB-C breakout in the back wall, wired straight to the display's cable. Shell and
  base.
- **USB-C and 18650** (`DESKLING_PORT=usbc DESKLING_POWER=18650`): the same shell on a 25 mm tray that holds an 18650
  and an IP5306 charge board, so the stand runs off the desk. Shell, deck and tray.
- **Cable** (`DESKLING_PORT=cable`): the display's own MX1.25-to-USB cable, uncut, leaves through a slot at the bottom
  of the back wall and is held by snap clips in the base. No soldering. Shell and base.

| `crowpanel_v7_shell_usbc.step` / `.stl` | The USB-C shell (65 × 92 × 54 mm), for both USB-C versions. The display presses in from below through a tilted bore and stops on a printed shoulder; a collar hides everything below its black grip band. Translucent PETG carries the display's LED skirt out as a glow. |
| `crowpanel_v7_base_usbc.step` / `.stl` | Base for the USB-C version: three posts under the display, pins for the breakout, cable clips. |
| `crowpanel_v7_deck_18650.step` / `.stl` | The same plate for the 18650 version, with plain screw holes and a slot for the charge board's wires. |
| `crowpanel_v7_tray_18650.step` / `.stl` | The tray under the deck (65 × 91 × 25 mm): an 18650 cradle with contact-plate slots (protected cells up to 69 mm), rails for the IP5306, a window in the right side for its LEDs and button, four screw columns. |
| `crowpanel_v7_shell_cable.step` / `.stl` | The cable shell: a 4.4 mm slot, open at the bottom, in the back wall. |
| `crowpanel_v7_base_cable.step` / `.stl` | Base for the cable shell: the same three posts, and four snug clips along the cable's run. |
| `v6/`, `v5/`, `v4/`, `v3/` | Earlier versions. |

The base fixes to the shell with 4 × M3×8 countersunk screws into heat-set inserts; on the 18650 version, 4 × M3×30
socket-head screws go up through the tray's columns and the deck into the same inserts. No part needs supports.
The STEP files share one frame (desk at z = 0, the front toward -Y; the tray sits below it) and line up when
imported together; the STLs are laid out to print.

## What changed in v7

The 18350 is gone: the IP5306 charges at up to about 2 A, with no way to turn it down, which is too much for a cell
that size. The USB-C shell loses its charge-board bay and button hole, and the USB-C base its cradle and rails. The
battery moves to an optional tray under the deck, sized for an 18650 that takes a 2 A charge, with the IP5306 beside
it and one window in the tray's right side for its charge LEDs and button. Everything else is v6.

## What changed in v6

From a printed v5:

- **No retainer.** Screwed at its front ear only, it pulled away from the display at the back, and the display is
  already held well as a press fit in the shell. Three posts on the base now stand under the plain outer rim of
  the display's bottom plate (r 28.9–30 mm; the screw heads inside it stand 0.7 mm proud and are cleared by
  0.35 mm), one at the front and one each side, so it can't be pushed down into the stand. The posts rise into
  pockets in the shell as the base goes on. `STOP_GAP` sets how far below the plate they finish (0 = touching).
- **Collar.** In v5 the collar was a separate solid floating 0.3 mm above the cup rim: the lip moved up and the
  collar's base didn't, so slicers bridged a gap there and the wall round the groove printed badly. The collar
  now grows out of the cup at full radius, the wall round the lit skirt is 2.45 mm all the way up, and the
  outside groove is gone. Internal dimensions are unchanged.
- **Rear screw bosses.** The two at the back floated free of the shell in v4 and v5 (separate solids inside the
  rear cavity). They now run up to the roof.
- **Shell:** USB-C receptacle pocket and plug opening +0.25 mm a side; IP5306 button hole 1.6 mm further back
  (`KEY_IN` 5.1).
- **Base:** cradle 2 mm longer for protected 18350 cells (the extra goes forward, away from the breakout); 7 ×
  6.5 mm solder windows behind each battery contact; buttresses behind both contact walls, the rear inner one kept
  0.8 mm off the breakout's edge; the USB-C version's wire clips are now the same snug clips as the cable version,
  for the display's bare 4 mm cable (v5's were drawn for a 6 mm bundle); the breakout pins stand 1.5 mm proud, to
  melt over with a soldering iron; no pocket under the IP5306 button (it is SMD, top side).

## Electronics

USB-C version: a 22 × 13 mm USB-C breakout (VBUS/GND/CC1/D-/D+/CC2, 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs fitted) at the rear port,
wired to the display's MX1.25-to-USB cable with its USB end cut off: VBUS → red (pin 1), D− → white (2), D+ → green
(3), GND → black (4) at J37, the display's USB connector, which goes straight to the ESP32-S3's USB pins. CC1/CC2
unconnected. The port powers and flashes the display.

18650 version: add an IP5306 charge + 5 V boost board (HW-775, 25 × 16 mm) and an 18650 with 18650-holder contact
plates, both in the tray. Breakout VBUS/GND → IP5306 VIN/GND; IP5306 BAT/GND → cell +/−; IP5306 OUT-5V/GND → the
cable's red and black; the breakout's D−/D+ still go straight to white and green. Pick a cell rated for a 2 A charge.
The IP5306 switches itself off under about 50 mA of load; firmware 5.21 keeps the Wi-Fi radio awake while the dial
naps to stay above it.

The breakout sits on two pins; melt their tops over its holes. The IP5306 has no holes: double-sided foam tape
between its rails holds it and keeps its underside off the tray.

## Measure first

`STOP_A` (7.0 mm) is the height of the display's fixed base above its bottom plate, taken from Elecrow's 3D model,
which has been wrong around the skirt before. It sets both the shoulder in the shell and the height of the posts in
the base: measure yours before printing. The display's cable is 4.0 mm across (`CABLE2_D`, measured); if yours differs, change it: the clips are drawn
to grip it 0.05 mm under size. For the USB-C version, the board sizes come from seller listings (±1–2 mm): check
the breakout's hole spacing (`BRK_HOLE_X`) and the IP5306 button's position (`KEY_IN`) against yours.

The website shows the parts in 3D (`site/pages/wiki/models.html`) and covers printing, wiring and assembly
(`site/pages/wiki/print.html`).
