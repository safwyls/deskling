Your pet, on your screen.

Deskling for Windows, macOS and Linux puts your companion in a corner of your desktop, above your other windows if you like. Click it for the ring of activities, right-click for the one you use most, and let it remind you about water, your eyes, stretching and moving while you work.

Get the source

Windows 10 or newer, macOS 10.15 or newer, or Linux

It isn’t in any app store and the installers aren’t signed, so Windows and macOS ask before running it the first time. The steps for each are below, and the source is in the repository if you’d rather build it yourself.

Install it

Windows

  1. Run Deskling-setup.exe. If SmartScreen says it protected your PC, choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Let it finish. It installs for you alone, with no administrator prompt, and starts.

macOS

  1. Open Deskling.dmg and drag Deskling into Applications.
  2. Open it once from Applications. macOS says it can’t check it for malicious software. Close that, open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down and choose Open Anyway.

Linux

  1. On Debian or Ubuntu, install deskling.deb with sudo apt install ./deskling.deb.
  2. Anywhere else, use Deskling.AppImage: make it executable (chmod +x Deskling.AppImage) and run it.

Living with it

  • Click your companion for the ring: log water or a walk, start an eye break or a stretch, play, put it to bed, pause the reminders. Click it again to pat it.
  • Right-click runs a shortcut: a pat to start with, or any of the activities.
  • Drag it anywhere. It stays where you leave it.
  • The tray icon (the menu bar on a Mac) holds the rest: its size, whether it stays above other windows, which companion, the reminders, and starting when you log in. It doesn’t show in the taskbar or the Dock.

Moving between devices

A companion lives in one place at a time. To bring yours over from the phone app, choose Give to a device there (from a dial, bring it to the phone first). You get a link: open it on the computer and choose Open in the Deskling app, or copy it and choose Take in a companion from a copied link from the tray menu.

Linux and Wayland

The tray icon needs a desktop with a system tray: GNOME needs the AppIndicator extension. On Wayland it runs through XWayland so it can keep its place and stay above other windows.
