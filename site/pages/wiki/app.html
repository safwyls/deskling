The phone app

Deskling for Android is where your companion goes when you leave the desk. Bring it along from the dial, look after it on the phone (it counts your steps toward Move and reminds you itself), and leave it at a dial again, the same one or another.

Install

The app isn’t in the Play Store. Download it from this site, which also walks through installing it, or build it yourself from app/ in the repository with Android Studio or ./gradlew :app:assembleRelease. It needs Android 8 or newer.

Pair it with your dial

  1. On the dial: Settings > Phone app > Pair a phone. It shows a 6-digit code.
  2. In the app: Settings > Pair a dial. Enter the code.
  3. Pick your dial from the Bluetooth list or the Wi-Fi list (either works; the code is needed both ways), then Pair.

A dial remembers up to four phones, and the app can be paired with several dials (Settings > Pair another): one at work and one at home, say. Settings > Phone app on the dial can forget every phone.

Your companion travels

The companion lives on one device at a time, and only that device shows it. Its level, streak and today’s breaks go with it.

  • Leaving the desk: on the app’s pet tab, Bring Wisp with me. The dial shows the clock and “Wisp is with Pixel 8”, and keeps its reminders and lights quiet until it’s back.
  • At a dial: Leave Wisp at Deskling-3456, for any dial you’ve paired. If that dial has a companion of its own, the app asks before replacing it.
  • To another phone: Give Wisp to another phone makes a link to send any way you like. Wisp moves in on the phone that opens it (the app asks first), and from there onto that person’s dials. Until then you can send the link again or take Wisp back. The link carries Wisp itself, so whoever opens it gets it: send it only to the phone it’s meant for.

Moves need the phone to reach the dial: over Bluetooth nearby, or over Wi-Fi on the same network. Each move counts up, so if a dial missed some (Wisp went to another dial and back to the phone, say), the next sync tells it where Wisp is now.

Which companion it is (Wisp, Crumb or Sprig) is a setting wherever it lives: Settings > Companion in the app, or on the dial. Its progress stays the same; a dial restarts to switch.

What it does

  • Everything on one screen. Tap your companion and the activities open in a wheel around it: water, eye break, stretch, move, sit or stand, play, bedtime and pause, with whatever is due lit up in its colour. Tap the companion again for a pat. Leaving it at a dial or giving it to another phone are the two buttons along the bottom.
  • One companion, wherever it is. On the phone, the phone runs the care game. At a dial, the dial does, and the app shows where it is and how it’s doing.
  • Instant sync. While you’re nearby, the app keeps a Bluetooth link open. What you do on either side reaches the other within a second or two, and the app’s header says “in sync”. Android shows a quiet notification while it’s on; Settings > Stay connected to the dial turns it off.
  • Steps feed Move. Walk 250 steps within ten minutes and the app logs a Move for you. Both numbers are in Settings.
  • Reminders on the phone while your companion is with you, each with a Done button. Settings > Reminders on the phone can also post them while it’s at a dial and you’re away from it. With the companion on the phone, the edge of the screen also glows in the need’s colour, like the dial’s light ring (it needs Android’s “display over other apps” permission; Settings > Reminders turns it on or off).
  • The dial’s settings from your phone, including its Wi-Fi.
  • Your companion’s colours, and its face on the app icon.
  • A home screen widget: your companion in its current pose, how it’s doing, what’s due next and today’s steps. When you add it you pick what goes behind it: a panel in your companion’s colours, frosted glass, or nothing at all. Change it later in Settings > Phone.
