Print the stand

The stand is the only thing you make: a shell that holds the display tipped 30° toward you, a retainer ring that clamps it in from below, and a base plate that closes the bottom. In translucent filament the shell picks up the display’s LEDs as a glow around its edge.

The parts

PartSizeWhat it does
Shell65 × 91 × 53 mmThe body you see. The display goes in from below through a tilted bore and stops against a printed shoulder; a collar at the top hides everything below its black grip band.
Retainer60 × 66 × 12 mmA ring that pushes the display’s bottom plate up against the shoulder. Two screws through its front ear hold it.
Base65 × 91 × 18 mmA 3 mm floor plate with clips for the USB-C pigtail and its cable, and a cradle for an 18350 cell.

Turn them around, see them pulled apart, and download STEP or STL on The 3D files.

Material

We printed the shell in translucent PETG. The display’s lit skirt, the ring of LEDs that turns with the knob, sits inside the shell just under its collar, so a clear or frosted filament carries its colour out around the display’s edge: blue when water is due, amber when it’s time for an eye break. Opaque filament works fine too; you just lose the glow. The retainer and base are hidden, so print them in whatever you have.

Settings to start from

These are sensible defaults for functional PETG parts, not a tuned profile. Adjust for your printer.

SettingStart withWhy
OrientationAs the STL files come: shell and base on their flat bottoms, the retainer flange downEach part’s biggest flat face is already down.
Layer height0.2 mmFine for parts this size.
Walls3 or 4The shell is mostly 2.2 mm wall. More walls also diffuse the LED light more evenly.
Infill15–20%Enough for the screw bosses and the base’s clips.
SupportsCheck your slicer’s previewThe roof inside the shell’s sloped top and the stop shoulder in its bore are overhangs. The retainer’s front is trimmed flat to sit on the base, so with the flange down it hangs up to about 5 mm above the bed: give it supports from the build plate.

Putting it together

  1. Inserts. Melt four M3 heat-set inserts into the bosses on the shell’s underside.
  2. Display. Feed the display up into the shell from underneath, screen first, until its base band stops against the printed shoulder. The black grip band comes out through the collar at the top.
  3. Retainer. Push the retainer ring up under the display’s bottom plate and drive two M3 screws up through its front ear into the shell. The ring is drawn slightly proud, so tightening the screws preloads the display against the stop and it doesn’t rattle.
  4. Power. Seat the USB-C breakout between the rails at the back of the base, with its socket facing the port in the shell’s back wall, run the cable through the clips, and let it bend up through the hole in the floor to the display.
  5. Base. Close it up with four M3 countersunk screws from below, into the inserts.

Flash the firmware before you close the base. The display’s own USB-C port and its BOOT and RESET buttons are on its underside: the retainer’s open middle leaves them reachable, the base covers them.
