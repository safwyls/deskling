Print the ring

The ring is the only part you make. It grips the display’s body and holds it tipped 30° toward you, and in translucent filament it turns the LEDs into a soft glow on your desk.

The model

One solid part, about 60 × 62 × 43 mm. Its opening is sized for the CrowPanel’s rear body (about 52 mm across), with a lip that stops the display from sliding through; the 60 mm knob rests on top. The tab at the front carries two holes.

Material

We printed it in translucent PETG. The knob’s LED ring sits just above the printed part, so a clear or frosted filament carries its colour down into the ring: blue when water is due, amber when it’s time for an eye break. Opaque filament works fine too; you just lose the glow.

Settings to start from

These are sensible defaults for a small, round, functional part, not a tuned profile. Adjust for your printer.

SettingStart withWhy
OrientationRing lying flat (its opening facing up)Round walls print round; the layer lines run around the ring rather than across it.
Layer height0.2 mmFine for a part this size.
Walls3 or 4A ring is mostly wall. More walls also diffuse the LED light more evenly.
Infill15–20%Only the tab and the base have any.

Fit

Push the display’s body into the ring from the top until the knob sits on the rim. It should hold without glue. If it’s too tight, scale the model up by 0.5% in X and Y and print again; if it rattles, scale down by the same.

Leave the back of the display reachable: you’ll want the USB-C port, and the BOOT and RESET buttons if a flash ever needs a nudge.
