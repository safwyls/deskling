Print the stand

The stand is the only thing you make. It’s two parts: a shell that sits on your desk, and a bracket hidden inside it that holds the display tipped 30° toward you. In translucent filament the shell turns the knob’s LEDs into a soft glow.

The models

PartSizeWhat it does
ShellAbout 65 × 91 × 53 mm, 33 cm³ if solidThe part you see. Its top is a face tipped 30° toward you with a round opening; the knob rests on the rim around it.
BracketAbout 60 × 63 × 43 mm, 7 cm³ if solidSits inside the shell and grips the display’s rear body (about 52 mm across), with a lip that stops it sliding through. The tab at the front carries two holes.
  • STEP and STL files for both parts, in the repository’s hardware/ folder

Material

We printed the shell in translucent PETG. The knob’s LED ring sits just above the printed part, so a clear or frosted filament carries its colour down into the shell: blue when water is due, amber when it’s time for an eye break. Opaque filament works fine too; you just lose the glow.

Settings to start from

These are sensible defaults for functional PETG parts, not a tuned profile. Adjust for your printer.

SettingStart withWhy
OrientationShell on its flat base, as modelledThe base is the one big flat face. The inside of the tipped top is an overhang, so look at it in the preview.
Layer height0.2 mmFine for a part this size.
Walls3 or 4Both parts are mostly wall. More walls on the shell also diffuse the LED light more evenly.
Infill15–20%Enough for the shell’s base and the bracket’s tab.
SupportsCheck your slicer’s previewThe shell’s inner ceiling, and the bracket’s tilted edge or tab, may want some depending on how you lay them.

Fit

The display’s body goes into the bracket until the knob sits on the shell’s rim. It should hold without glue. If it’s too tight, scale the model up by 0.5% in X and Y and print again; if it rattles, scale down by the same.

Leave the back of the display reachable: you’ll want the USB-C port, and the BOOT and RESET buttons if a flash ever needs a nudge.
