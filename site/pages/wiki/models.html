The 3D files

The stand is three printed parts. Turn them around, look inside, pull them apart, and download the ones you want to print.

Drag to turn, right-drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom. The display is a simple stand-in from Elecrow’s model.

Assembled, the stand is 65.5 × 90.7 × 56.1 mm. The STL files are laid out to print: the shell and base on their flat bottoms, the retainer flange down (its trimmed front needs a little support from the bed). The shell needs one painted-on support, under the ear seat in its front slot. Grams are for a solid part; with walls and infill you’ll use less. Printing and assembly covers the rest.

Screws and bits

  • 4 × M3 heat-set inserts in the shell’s bosses (4.0 mm holes, up to 6 mm deep), for the base.
  • 4 × M3×8 countersunk screws up through the base into those inserts.
  • 2 × M3×8 socket-head screws through the retainer’s ear, threaded straight into 2.6 mm pilot holes in the shell. Both are at the front: a slot at the back would have cut through the back wall at the USB port.
  • An inline USB-C breakout (the JRC-B008 kind, about 15 × 10 mm) and a 6 mm cable for the pigtail.

Changing the design

All three parts come from one parametric script, hardware/crowpanel_stand_v3.py, written with CadQuery. Every dimension is a named parameter at the top: the tilt, wall thickness, clearances, the cable run, the screw positions. Change one and run it:

pip install cadquery
python3 crowpanel_stand_v3.py

It writes STEP and STL for each part, and prints a set of checks first: the display’s path in from below, every part against the display and each other, the screwdriver path to the retainer, at least 1.15 mm of wall around every screw hole, and the cable and cell against the walls.

One number to check against your own display first: STOP_A, the height of the display’s fixed base above its bottom plate (7.0 mm in Elecrow’s model). See Print the stand.

All the parts, and the display, share one coordinate frame (desk at z = 0, the front toward −Y), so the shell and base STEP files, and the assembled retainer the script also writes, drop into any CAD program already in place.
