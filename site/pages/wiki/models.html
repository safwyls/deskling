The 3D files

The stand is two printed parts, a shell and a deck. It comes with a USB-C port in the back, or in an older version that uses the cable that came with your display, with no soldering. Turn them around, look inside, pull them apart, and download the ones you want to print.

Drag to turn, right-drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom. The display, the breakout and the cable are simple stand-ins.

Assembled, the stand is 65 × 65 × 56.5 mm. Grams are for a solid part; with walls and infill you’ll use less. The whole stand as one STEP assembly has both parts in place, with stand-ins for the display, the breakout and the cable. Printing and assembly covers the rest.

Screws and bits

  • 4 × M3 heat-set inserts in the shell (4.0 mm holes, 6 mm deep): two at the front, one on each side.
  • 4 × M3×8 countersunk screws up through the deck into those inserts.
  • A USB-C breakout board, 22 × 13 mm with VBUS, GND, CC1, D−, D+ and CC2 pads, and the display’s own MX1.25-to-USB cable with its USB end cut off. Wiring is in Print the stand.

The cable version

The version that keeps the display’s cable whole is still the version 7 design: a longer shell with a slot at the bottom of its back wall, and a base with four clips that grip the cable. It hasn’t had version 8’s changes. Its files are in hardware/v7: shell and base (STL; STEP alongside).

What changed in version 8

Version 8 was reworked by hand in CAD from version 7. The stand is 27 mm shorter front to back, 65 mm instead of 92. The back of the stand is now part of the deck: a curved back wall with the USB-C port in it, which slides up into the shell, located by two tall posts in sockets inside the shell. The USB-C breakout sits further forward, and the deck’s rear pair of screws moved to the sides.

Earlier versions

Version 7 was the last one generated by the parametric script. Version 6 replaced the retainer ring with three posts on the base that stand under the display, and fixed a collar that version 5 printed as a separate floating ring. Version 5 gave the knob room to turn freely and added the cable version. Every earlier version is in hardware/, each in its own folder.

Changing the design

The version 8 files were edited by hand, so the easiest place to change them is the STEP files, in any CAD program. All the parts and the display share one coordinate frame (desk at z = 0, the front toward −Y), so they drop into place together.

Version 7 and earlier come from a parametric script written with CadQuery, hardware/v7/crowpanel_stand_v7.py. Every dimension is a named parameter at the top, and it prints a set of fit and clash checks as it builds. It’s a good starting point for bigger changes, such as a display whose fixed base isn’t 7.0 mm tall (STOP_A).
