The 3D files

The stand is three printed parts, with room inside for a battery. Turn them around, look inside, pull them apart, and download the ones you want to print.

Drag to turn, right-drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom. The display, the two boards and the cell are simple stand-ins, shown green and grey.

Assembled, the stand is 65.5 × 91.1 × 56.1 mm. The STL files are laid out to print: the shell and base on their flat bottoms, the retainer flange down (its trimmed front needs a little support from the bed). The shell needs one painted-on support, under the ear seat in its front slot. Grams are for a solid part; with walls and infill you’ll use less. Printing and assembly covers the rest.

Screws and bits

  • 4 × M3 heat-set inserts in the shell’s bosses (4.0 mm holes, up to 6 mm deep), for the base.
  • 4 × M3×8 countersunk screws up through the base into those inserts.
  • 2 × M3×8 socket-head screws through the retainer’s ear, threaded straight into 2.6 mm pilot holes in the shell. Both are at the front: a slot at the back would have cut through the back wall at the USB port.
  • A USB-C breakout board, 22 × 13 mm with VBUS, GND, CC1, D−, D+ and CC2 pads, for the socket in the back.
  • For the battery: an IP5306 charge and boost board (HW-775, 25 × 16 mm), an 18350 cell, and a flat and a spring contact plate from an 18650 holder. Wiring is in Print the stand.

What changed in version 4

Version 3 took power through an inline USB-C pigtail and had an empty cradle for a cell. Version 4 makes the battery work: the back holds a USB-C breakout board on two pins, the right side holds an IP5306 charge and boost board with a hole in the wall for its button, and the wires run down the middle in the same clips. To fit the 22 mm breakout, the back of the shell is a little squarer, the plastic in front of the socket is 1 mm, and the two rear base screws moved 1 mm outwards. The retainer hasn’t changed, so a v3 retainer fits.

Changing the design

All three parts come from one parametric script, hardware/crowpanel_stand_v4.py, written with CadQuery. Every dimension is a named parameter at the top: the tilt, wall thickness, clearances, the cable run, the screw positions. Change one and run it:

pip install cadquery
python3 crowpanel_stand_v4.py

It writes STEP and STL for each part, and prints a set of checks first: the display’s path in from below, every part against the display and each other, the screwdriver path to the retainer, at least 1.15 mm of wall around every screw hole, the cable, the boards and the cell against the walls, and that the battery board’s button hole is clear.

One number to check against your own display first: STOP_A, the height of the display’s fixed base above its bottom plate (7.0 mm in Elecrow’s model). See Print the stand.

All the parts, and the display, share one coordinate frame (desk at z = 0, the front toward −Y), so the shell and base STEP files, and the assembled retainer the script also writes, drop into any CAD program already in place.
